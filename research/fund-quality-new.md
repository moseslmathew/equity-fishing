# Independent mutual-fund candidate research

Research cutoff: 12 October 2026, India evening. No attachment was opened or used. These are original notes based on retrieved company filings and independent reporting. A company appearing in a small-cap fund need not itself currently qualify as small-cap.

## eClerx Services — AI adoption with measurable commercial tests

**Verified evidence.** Q1 FY27 operating revenue was ₹1,152.4 crore, with constant-currency growth of 15.1% year on year. Operating EBITDA margin was 23.0%; PAT was ₹164.3 crore. Sequential profit declined, so strong top-line growth did not eliminate near-term investment pressure. The September 2 Investor Day, summarized by the company on September 28, describes AI-enabled operations, client transformation, embedded products and employee tools. Kapil Jain's team is moving toward outcome-based delivery; QA360 and agentic finance workflows illustrate what this means commercially. These are company descriptions, not independently audited claims of superior software quality.

**Investment interpretation.** Existing workflow knowledge can help eClerx implement AI where generic models alone do not solve data-access, compliance and operational-integration problems. The strongest version of the thesis is that clients buy better outcomes and eClerx retains part of the productivity gain. The adverse case is that clients capture savings through lower prices while the vendor incurs AI and sales costs. Assess management through repeat client expansion, realized cash conversion and revenue per employee—not the frequency of the word AI. An Emkay research summary dated September 3 was constructive on the operating direction yet retained REDUCE: useful evidence that a good business narrative does not settle valuation. Its judgment is an analyst view, not consensus.

**Decision gate.** Retain as a researched candidate, conditional on durable margins and cash conversion while growing. Value normalized cash earnings after AI investment; do not award a software-product multiple to the entire services business without evidence of scalable recurring product income. Named customer retention/independent satisfaction data were not sufficiently verified to assign a universal reputation score.

## KPIT Technologies — technology relevance does not remove customer-cycle risk

**Verified evidence.** KPIT's July 29 release reported Q1 FY27 constant-currency revenue growth of just 0.1% year on year, versus 8.9% in rupees; EBITDA margin was 17.2%, with $257 million of new engagement wins. Management acknowledged pressure at several large customers and expects stronger second-half growth. That recovery is guidance. The investor update reported ₹900 crore net cash and 51-day DSO. Its software-defined vehicle, diagnostics, cybersecurity and vehicle-integration work creates a plausible route to benefit from more software-intensive mobility; company production-program claims establish experience, not guaranteed future share.

**Why opinions differ.** Economic Times on September 30 reported substantial earnings-estimate cuts amid European automotive budget pressure. International evidence strengthens the caution: Reuters on October 12 described Volkswagen's weaker deliveries and intensifying European/Chinese competition; September 17 coverage of Xpeng expanding technology licensing shows that incumbents also face new software suppliers. These are sector conditions, not evidence that KPIT lost a specific contract. OEM pressure can increase demand for engineering efficiency while simultaneously delaying projects. AI is consequently both a productivity tool and a source of bargaining pressure.

**Decision gate.** Classify as a conditional recovery case rather than a highest-conviction growth selection. Kishor Patil's management should demonstrate conversion of wins into revenue, improved customer diversification and margin recovery. Qorix investment losses and customer concentration belong in the downside scenario. Value the company using revised, sustainable earnings and cash generation; a price fall alone does not establish cheapness. Neither YouTube enthusiasm nor technological demonstrations substitute for profitable production deployment. No specific new V2V mandate benefit is treated as booked revenue.

## Kalpataru Projects International — infrastructure demand must become collected cash

**Verified evidence.** The August 11 issuer release reported Q1 FY27 revenue of ₹6,408 crore, 4% growth, and EBITDA of ₹562 crore, 7% growth. Order backlog was ₹66,607 crore at June 30. The same release reported 80 working-capital days and ₹917 crore consolidated net debt, improved year on year. September 24 brought ₹2,025 crore of new awards; another oil-and-gas award followed September 28. Neither L1 status nor order backlog is recognized revenue. Do not confuse this listed EPC company with Kalpataru Limited, the property developer.

**Growth mechanism and quality.** Grid expansion, high-voltage transmission and complex substations provide exposure to electrification infrastructure without requiring a bet on a particular EV or renewable technology winner. Manish Mohnot's team cites HVDC, GIS and design-build capabilities. The moat is more plausibly prequalification, execution history, manufacturing capability and project controls than a consumer-facing brand. The consequence of an execution failure is tangible: delayed certification, retention money, cost overruns and receivables. Sector demand alone cannot compensate for poor contract terms.

**Decision gate.** The healthier working-capital position is a useful management signal, but investors should monitor whether cash discipline survives a larger project book. PAT growth substantially exceeded EBITDA growth, and NDTV Profit highlighted other-income support; avoid extrapolating headline profit growth into a recurring forecast. The bull case combines backlog conversion with controlled cash absorption. The bear case is strong accounting growth accompanied by delayed collections and overseas project risk. Value recurring operating earnings and free cash after project working capital, using realistic execution timelines rather than treating the backlog as equity value. This is an infrastructure execution case, not an AI-product stock.

## Narayana Hrudayalaya — clinical franchise, with expansion and insurance complexity

**Verified evidence.** The revised Q1 FY27 presentation filed August 3 reported consolidated revenue of ₹2,683.6 crore, EBITDA margin 18.8%, and PAT ₹207.3 crore. Headline growth includes the enlarged UK footprint; it is not a same-hospital growth rate. India revenue grew 16.6%. The presentation reports robotic surgery adoption and other advanced procedures, but these are deployment disclosures rather than controlled studies showing superior outcomes. Foreign-currency debt and the newer insurance business add complexity: Q1 insurance losses demonstrate that premium growth is not yet evidence of profitable underwriting.

**Future-worth interpretation.** The more defensible moat is experienced clinical teams, referral relationships and efficient delivery of complex treatment. Robotics and digital hospital systems can support clinical capabilities and throughput, but purchasing a surgical robot alone is not a moat; competitors can buy the equipment. The growth case requires doctors, utilization, service standards and cash returns on additional capacity to develop together. The September 23 company transplant update supports a history of complex care, but does not establish comparative survival rates or justify a universal patient-satisfaction claim.

**Decision gate.** Preserve as a quality candidate while separating mature Indian hospital economics from UK integration, Cayman operations and early insurance investment. Management's challenge is sustaining clinical and service quality across jurisdictions without allowing expansion to outrun operating control. Business Standard's post-results coverage captured investor concern about margin pressure despite rapid revenue growth. Value mature hospital cash flow separately from development-stage operations; a single headline P/E may hide both startup losses and acquisition risk. Monitor doctor retention, hospital-level cash returns, debt and insurance claims economics. Online patient reviews can identify issues worth investigating, but cannot provide an unbiased clinical-quality ranking.

## Ipca Laboratories — operating recovery, with a long-dated biosimilar option

**Verified evidence.** The August 13 primary results show Q1 FY27 consolidated revenue from operations ₹2,788.10 crore and owner-attributable PAT ₹401.89 crore, up 72%. The frequently circulated ₹422.81 crore figure is before associates and noncontrolling-interest adjustments, so it should not be used as owner earnings. EBITDA before forex and other income was ₹637.94 crore, with margin 22.88%. The June corporate presentation describes domestic branded formulations, vertically integrated APIs and an early biosimilar pipeline. Those development timelines and licensing plans are management's pipeline description, not approved products or assured sales.

**Growth and quality interpretation.** Existing domestic brands and distribution are more tangible supports than extrapolating distant biotechnology revenue. Vertical integration may improve manufacturing control and supply economics, but it also concentrates responsibility for consistent regulatory compliance. Unichem and other subsidiary operations require evaluation on their own cash returns rather than assuming acquisitions automatically improve business quality. Successful biosimilars would add an option on a larger future addressable market; clinical, regulatory and commercial development can also consume capital for years.

**Decision gate.** Keep the case grounded in recurring formulations demand, export execution and cash generation. Current growth supports an operating-recovery thesis, not evidence that every compliance risk has disappeared. Recent broker reporting raised FY27 expectations, but that raises the hurdle for subsequent delivery. Value owner earnings after normalizing forex and exceptional items; assign conservative probability-weighted value to pipeline assets. A complete current plant-by-plant FDA review was not established in this research, so no blanket clean-regulatory-bill claim is appropriate. Likewise, product-market presence and approvals do not prove that all products are superior to competitors.
